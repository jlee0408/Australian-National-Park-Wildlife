# Findings and interpretation notes

This document records the summary values supplied during project development. They have not been independently recalculated from the original source CSV files in the recovered archive. 

## Headline figures

* Threatened Species Index: 0.33 in 2021 relative to a 1985 baseline of 1.0, approximately a 67% decline.
* Natural woody vegetation: 114.5 million hectares in 2024.
* CAPAD protected-area dataset: 14,492 records representing 11,208 distinct PA IDs.
* Exotic environmental pest assessments: 167 species, including 43 flagged as higher-risk within their thematic group.

## M1 — Protected-area records

* Tasmania: 4,236 mapped records (29.2%).
* Western Australia: approximately 79.5 million hectares, or 45.2% of summed area.
* The map shows 14,471 mapped records from 14,492 source records; external-territory records are not shown on the state map.
* Records are database entries, not necessarily unique parks. Overlapping boundaries can inflate summed area.

## M2 — Protection status by IBRA region

* Supplied overall share: 26.7% of occurrence records inside protected areas.
* Examples supplied: Arnhem Plateau 90.7%; Tasmanian West 76.3%; Sturt Plateau 4.8%; Mount Isa Inlier 4.9%.
* This is a share of records, not the percentage of land area protected. Confirm that the figure matches the selected map measure before publication.

## M3 — Threatened species associated with protected-area locations

* Supplied 2016–2020 counts: Sydney Basin 179; South Eastern Queensland 168; NSW North Coast 109; South Eastern Highlands 106.
* Of 14,471 mapped locations, 13,248 (91.5%) were matched to an IBRA region; 118 species names were classified as threatened in the supplied summary.
* These are regional counts associated with locations, not park-specific surveys or counts of individual animals.

## C4 and C5 — Land cover

* Grassland share: 36.9% in 1988 to 44.2% in 2024.
* Herbaceous crop share: 17.8% in 1988 to 8.8% in 2024.
* A large 2011 grassland spike was reportedly substantially reversed in 2012. Check the source methodology before offering a causal explanation.
* Queensland natural woody vegetation: 38.4 million hectares (33.5%) in 2024; supplied national total 114.5 million hectares.
* Area is not, by itself, a measure of ecological condition or legal protection.

## C6 — Threatened Species Index

* 1985 baseline: 1.0.
* Supplied 2021 value: 0.33, with a supplied range of 0.17–0.68.
* This is a relative index for monitored species, not a census of every threatened species or individual animal.

## C7 — Waterbirds

* Supplied combined-program peak: approximately 4.84 million birds in 2008, associated with a one-off national survey.
* Survey programs, coverage, effort and conditions can differ. Confirm the aggregation and survey metadata before interpreting the value as a population trend.

## C8 and C9 — Monitoring records

* eBird Australia: approximately 42.4 million records (62.4%); the top five resources account for 87.3% of records.
* South Australia: approximately 91.6% of the supplied D7 regional monitoring-effort records.
* Record totals indicate reporting/monitoring activity, not wildlife abundance. C8 and C9 use different subsets and should not be compared as if they were the same measure.

## C10 — Exotic environmental pests

* 43 of 167 species (25.7%) were flagged as higher-risk within their group.
* 44 of 167 (26.3%) had Major/Massive environmental-impact ratings.
* The higher-risk flag and environmental-impact rating are separate fields. Preserve the source's categories; do not create an unsupported combined score. An assessment is not proof of an outbreak or sighting.
